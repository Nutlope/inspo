The Kitchen

A menu you don't order.

I — Philosophy

There is one menu each evening and you will eat all of it. We think that is the kindest thing a kitchen can offer: a single, finished thought, served in the order it was meant to be read.

Lucia Maranzano grew up in the Langhe, an hour south, where the fog sits in the vineyards until noon. She cooks the way her grandmother set a table — quietly, completely, and without asking what you'd prefer.

The pantry is small and local on purpose: hazelnuts from Cortemilia, beef raised in the Po valley, herbs and roots foraged in the Alpine foothills the same morning they are plated. What isn't good that day simply doesn't appear.

Each course is a single sentence — one protein, one season, one idea — and the eleven of them together make a paragraph. The room is dark so that the plate, and the person across from you, are the only things lit.

Lucia Maranzano Chef · Patron

The Room

Fourteen seats, one evening.

III — The experience

We seat the whole room within twenty minutes so the kitchen can cook for everyone at once. What you give up in flexibility you get back in temperature, timing, and quiet.

i.

A single table

Fourteen chairs around one long oak table, lit by tapers. You will likely speak to the people beside you. Most nights, that becomes the point.

No à la carte · No bar seating
ii.

Cooked, not plated

An open pass at the end of the room. You watch the eleventh course finished by hand, by the same people who foraged it that morning.

Counter view on request
iii.

Roughly three hours

Allow the full evening — the menu is paced, not rushed. The early seating ends in time for the opera; the late one runs as long as it wants to.

Phones kept in the drawer, please
