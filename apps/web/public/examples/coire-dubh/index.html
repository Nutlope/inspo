Distilling on the Sound of Sleat since 1884

Two stills, one warehouse, and no hurry at all.

We fill about four hundred casks a year and lose roughly two percent of each to the air. Everything we bottle has been asleep in this building for at least twelve winters, and we will tell you exactly which cask yours came out of.

Copper stills, both originals

Three decisions, made the same way since 1884.

Everything else here is modern. These three are not, because we tried the modern version and preferred the old one.

Water

Off the hill, not the mains

The burn runs peaty and soft over granite. In a dry August we stop distilling rather than switch source, which costs us about three weeks most years.

Ferment

Seventy-two hours, in wood

Six Oregon pine washbacks, long ferments, and a house yeast we have kept going since the 1970s. Stainless would be easier to clean and would taste like it.

Cut

Made by nose, on the hour

The stillman decides where the middle cut starts and stops. Two people hold that job and they disagree slightly, which is why casks from alternating weeks taste different.

The warehouse is open on Thursdays.

Six people at a time, ninety minutes, and you taste straight from the cask with a valinch. There is no gift shop. There is a bench outside facing the water, which is better.
