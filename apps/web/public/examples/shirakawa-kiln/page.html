The kiln

Five days of fire, twice a year.

The anagama at Ōhara is a single-chamber tunnel kiln built into the slope in 2016. We stoke it with red pine for about 110 hours, then leave it sealed for a week to cool. Nothing is glazed: the colour is ash from the fire settling on bare clay, and the side of a pot that faced the flame will never match the side that did not.

110hours of stoking, in shifts of four
1,300degrees at the front of the chamber

Clay bodies

Three clays, no glaze.

  • Shigaraki blend

    Coarse and pale, with feldspar that bursts white through the surface in the fire. The clay for cups and flasks, because it is kind to the lips and light in the hand.

    Yunomi, tokkuri, small plates

  • Kyoto red

    A fine, iron-rich clay from the Fushimi side of the city, fired to a deep brick. At the front of the kiln it takes a green ash flash that we cannot plan for and would not want to.

    Chawan, faceted bowls

  • Iron-flecked stoneware

    Our own mix, with crushed iron sand from the Kamo river bed. Dark, heavy and flecked where the iron melts out. It holds the largest forms without slumping.

    Jars, hanaire, rim plates
A glaze is a decision made before the fire. We would rather let the fire decide.

Mio Tanabe, on why the studio stopped glazing in 2019

Mio Tanabe

Potter, founder

Trained at the prefectural ceramics school in Kiyomizu and apprenticed for six years in Shigaraki before opening the Jōdoji studio in 2014. Throws every piece that goes into the kiln, and rejects more of them than she keeps.

Kenji Arai

Kiln, wood, accounts

Built the Ōhara anagama by hand over two summers with a borrowed excavator and a great deal of advice. Splits and stacks the pine a year ahead, runs the firing schedule, and keeps the studio cat, Sumi, fed.
