Nº 02 - The Family

Eight weights, two italics, one voice.

Caxton is drawn across a full weight axis - from a hairline Thin (100) to a near-black Black (900) - each with a true italic, not a slanted roman. The italics carry their own drawing: a single-story a, a swash f, and a descending tail on the Q that clears the line below.

Nº 03 - The Drawing

Three years in the details.

Every curve in Caxton was redrawn at least four times. The result is a serif that holds its nerve at twelve pixels and breathes at a hundred and twenty - with the small optical sizes doing the quiet work in between.

a

A single-story italic

The italic a drops its upper bowl entirely - a choice that traces back to the chancery hands Caxton's namesake set in type in 1476. It earns its keep in running text, where the roman a and its italic sibling stay legibly distinct at body size.

g

A closed-loop descender

The g carries a closed lower loop with a subtle ear - drawn so the counter never collapses under sub-pixel rendering. Tested against ClearType, Retina, and a 1998 CRT we keep in the studio for humility.

Q

A tail that clears the line

The Q's tail sweeps below the baseline but stops short of the descender line - so it never collides with the row beneath in tight leading. The italic version adds a swash that would make a Venetian printer blush.
