The evening

Everyone arrives at the same time, and nobody is hurried out.

On the single seating

Doors open at quarter past seven. At half past, the first course goes to all fourteen seats at once, and from then on the room moves as one table would. There is no second sitting waiting for your chairs, so the evening ends when it ends, usually a little before eleven.

On the kitchen

Three of us cook, and you can see all three from every seat. Ingredients come from a short list of people we have worked with for years: a grower in Kent, a day boat out of Newlyn, a dairy on the Somerset Levels. The menu is written for the season and adjusted on the morning of service to what actually arrived.

On quiet

We do not play music. Plates are set down and described briefly, then left alone. Phones are welcome for a photograph, and we ask that they rest face down after that. Most guests tell us it is the first time in a while they have talked to the person they came with for three hours.

Wine

Six pours, poured when the plate they belong to arrives.

The pairing is written after the menu, never before it. We favour producers who farm their own vines, and we pour smaller measures than most so that the last glass is still a pleasure. Bottles from the same list are available by the glass if you would rather choose your own.

The list itself runs to about a hundred and forty bottles, weighted towards the Loire, Burgundy, the Jura and the Mosel, with a short page of English sparkling wine that we are quietly proud of.

Bring a bottle if you like. Corkage is £30, waived when you also take the pairing.

Reservations

How a seat is taken, and what we ask in return.

We release seats two months ahead, and they usually go within the week. Everything below is the whole arrangement; there is no small print elsewhere.

  1. 1

    Seats are released on the first of the month

    At 10:00 on the first of each month we open every evening for the month after next. November evenings, for instance, were released on 1 September.

  2. 2

    A deposit of £75 per person holds the seat

    Taken by card at the time of booking and deducted from your bill on the night. Tables are for one to four guests; the six-seat window table can be taken whole by writing to us.

  3. 3

    Cancel seven days ahead for a full return

    Inside seven days the deposit is kept, unless we manage to fill your seat from the waiting list, in which case it comes back to you in full. We will always try.

  4. 4

    Tell us what you cannot eat when you book

    Vegetarian menus need five days' notice. Guests must be twelve or over. If your party will be late, telephone: we can hold the first course for ten minutes, not longer, because the whole room waits with you.

The room

A bookbinder's workshop, kept mostly as we found it.

The building bound ledgers and hymnals from 1904 until 1987. We kept the north-light window, the cast-iron columns and the long ash bench that became the counter. Eight guests sit at the counter, facing the pass; six sit at the window table, which is shared unless a party takes it whole.

Walls are limewashed the colour of the original paper stock, and the only light after dark is from the pass and the candles on the counter. It is a warm room in winter and, thanks to the window, a cool one in September.
