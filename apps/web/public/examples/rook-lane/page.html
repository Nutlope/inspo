Pressing notes

How a Rook Lane record is made.

Five steps · no digital stage at any of them

Every record in the series follows the same route from the vault to your turntable. It is slower and dearer than the usual way, which is rather the point.

01

Tape transfer

We borrow the original master reels, never a digital copy. Each reel is baked, cleaned and played once, in the room where the cut will be made.

Studer A80, 15 inches per secondMono in, mono out. Stereo kept only where the session was recorded that way.
02

Cutting

The lacquer is cut straight from the tape. Sides are kept under twenty-two minutes so the grooves stay wide and the inner bands do not pinch.

Neumann VMS-70 lathe, Kessel Mastering, BerlinCut by Anneliese Ruhl. Test acetates played against an original pressing.
03

Plating

Lacquers reach the plant within forty-eight hours of the cut. One father, one mother, and a fresh stamper for every five hundred copies.

Three-step platingStampers retired at 500 pulls. Matrix numbers hand-scribed in the run-out.
04

Pressing

One hundred and eighty gram virgin vinyl, pressed slowly and cooled flat on the platter. Every hundredth copy is played end to end before it is sleeved.

180 g, edition of 1,500One in a hundred test-played. Rejects are ground down, not sold as seconds.
05

Sleeve

The original artwork is reset in the same proportions and printed in two blacks on uncoated board with a tip-on finish. Nothing is laminated.

Tip-on board, two blacksSleeve notes reprinted in full, plus a new four-page insert on the session.

RL-014 · out 24 September

Northbound, sixty years on.

Recorded over two March days in 1966 at Van Doren Studio in Newark, Northbound was Lionel Okafor's only date as a leader. Marlowe Records pressed three thousand copies, the label folded the following spring, and the tapes went into a bank vault with the rest of the estate.

We found them in 2024, boxed and labelled in Okafor's own hand, with a note asking that the record be kept in mono. It has been. Ruhl's cut sits a touch quieter than the 1966 pressing so the bass has somewhere to go, and the fade on The long platform now runs its full twenty seconds instead of being lopped for the side length.

The sleeve is reset from the original mechanicals, with Okafor's name moved up so it no longer clips the rules. The insert carries session photographs by Ruth Alder, the alternate take sheets, and a new note by the trumpeter's daughter, Adaeze Okafor.
