The programme

Select a production for the synopsis and creative team

  1. Opening night, Thursday 4 December

    Ashdown's first full-length opera takes the last hour of the Tristan legend and turns it around: Isolde wakes before the ship lands, alone on the headland, and the whole score is what she decides to do with the morning. A chamber chorus of twelve stands in for the sea. Commissioned by Ravensgate with the Harlow Trust.

    ConductorTeodora Vasić
    DirectorCallum Ferrier
    IsoldeNkechi Adeyemi
    BrangäneLise Hallström
    DesignStudio Morrow
  2. First Ravensgate staging since 1994

    Loredan's grand melodrama of a Venetian widow who marries a glassblower to keep her title, then learns his furnace is heating the city's rumours. Anja Solberg's production moves the action into a single hall of mirrors that slowly fills with smoke across the three acts. The famous second-act sextet is sung unamplified from the stalls.

    ConductorMarcus Oyelaran
    DirectorAnja Solberg
    The DuchessRosalind Achterberg
    PieroDmitri Kalashnik
    Chorus masterHelen Bray
  3. Five nights only

    Nine songs, nine rivers, one winter walk across a country that no longer exists. Rell wrote the cycle for baritone Anders Lund in 2019; this is its first performance with the full Ravensgate Orchestra and a 60-voice chorus placed in the grand circle. Lighting by Ines Quiroga, no set, no costumes.

    ConductorTeodora Vasić
    BaritoneAnders Lund
    LightingInes Quiroga
    ChorusRavensgate Chorus
  4. Returning after a sold-out 2022 run

    A shipyard on the Scheldt, a strike that lasts a winter, and two sisters on opposite sides of the gate. Verheyen's score sets a brass band against a string quartet and lets neither win. Revived with the original cast and Tomasz Wróbel's steel-plate set, which the company has kept in storage for four years for exactly this.

    ConductorMarcus Oyelaran
    DirectorFemke de Ruiter
    MartheSaskia Vermeulen
    LienNkechi Adeyemi
    SetTomasz Wróbel
  5. Under 16s £12 in every seat

    The night the city switches to electric light, one lamplighter refuses to go home. Kane's score borrows from music hall and brass-band hymns; Sato's libretto gives the streetlamps their own chorus. Ninety minutes, one interval, and a children's chorus of forty drawn from the Ravensgate Saturday school.

    ConductorHelen Bray
    DirectorCallum Ferrier
    The LamplighterOwen Pryce
    The MayorLise Hallström
    PuppetryLittle Iron Theatre

Opening the season · world premiere

Isolde at Dawn

Mireille Ashdown has spent four years on a score that lasts one morning. There is no Tristan on stage. There is a woman, a headland, and the question of whether to go down to the harbour at all.

"Every other version of this story ends with her dying beautifully. I wanted to know what she would do with a cup of coffee and a decision."

Mireille Ashdown, composer

Doors open one hour before curtain.
Latecomers are seated at the first suitable break. The foyer bar serves from 6pm; pre-order interval drinks at the cloakroom.

Pre-performance talks at 6.30pm, free with any ticket.
Backstage tours on Saturdays at 11am, £9. The Ashdown open rehearsal is on Sunday 30 November.

1,140 seats, four levels, one central aisle

The auditorium was rebuilt in 1931 as a horseshoe, so even the back of the upper circle sits within thirty metres of the conductor. Sightlines in the amphitheatre are full for everything except the far corners of the stalls-level set.
