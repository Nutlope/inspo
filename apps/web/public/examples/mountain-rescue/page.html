How to call for help

Do not walk to us. Do not message us. Ring 999 and we will come to you.

The police hold our pagers. Calling them is the only way to reach the team on the hill, and it is free from any phone, even one with no credit.

999

Ask for the police, then ask for mountain rescue.

112 works the same way. If you are abroad or your phone shows no network, either number will still try every mast it can reach.

  1. 01

    Stop, get warm, and stay with the casualty

    Put on every layer you have and get off the wind. Sit the casualty on a rucksack. A cold casualty deteriorates faster than an injured one.

  2. 02

    Dial 999, ask for police, then mountain rescue

    The operator will connect you to the police control room, and they page us. If the first call drops, ring again. Do not rely on a text.

  3. 03

    Tell them where you are, as precisely as you can

    A six-figure grid reference is best. A what3words address or a named feature is fine. Say the fell, the path and the last thing you passed.

    If you have signal, the police will send a text with a link. Open it. It sends us your phone's position. Say yes when they ask if you will accept it.

  4. 04

    Tell them what happened and who is with you

    Number in the party, the injury, whether the casualty is conscious and breathing, and what kit you have. Your phone number, said slowly.

  5. 05

    Keep the phone free and stay where you are

    We will ring you back, possibly more than once. Turn the screen brightness down to save the battery. When you hear us, shout or flash a torch, six flashes then a pause.

  6. 06

    No signal at all

    Register your phone for emergency texts before you go: text register to 999 and follow the reply. Then a text to 999 gets through on a signal too weak for a call.

    Failing that, send the strongest two people to the nearest road or house with a written note of the location, the time and the injury.

Volunteer training · a year to the callout list

Nobody joins the team and goes on the hill the same month.

Probationers train with the full team every Tuesday evening and one weekend a month. Twelve months and a pass at each stage earns a pager.

01January

Application and hill day

A written application, a chat at the base, then a day on Kettle Pike in whatever the weather is doing. We are looking for fitness, judgement and whether you are still cheerful at 4 in the afternoon.

02February to April

Navigation, day and night

Map, compass and pacing to a grid reference in cloud, then the same in the dark. Assessed on a night exercise across Ravens Edge with no head torch for the last leg.

03May

Casualty care

A four-day course and written exam covering fractures, head injuries, hypothermia, pain relief and the drugs we carry. Renewed every three years for the whole team.

04June to August

Rope and stretcher

Lowering systems, crag rescue and the stretcher over rough ground. Most of the summer is spent on Scarth Crag learning to be the one on the end of the rope.

05September to October

Search, radios and dogs

How a search is planned, how to run a line and what to say on a radio so the control vehicle can hear you. Working with the search dogs and their handlers on live exercises.

06November

Assessment and the pager

A full-day exercise with a scripted casualty, watched by the training officer and a member from a neighbouring team. Pass, and you are on the callout list from December.

What we ask of you

  • Live or work within 30 minutes of Harrowdale village
  • Be able to carry a 15 kg pack over the fell for a full day
  • Tuesday evenings and one weekend a month, all year
  • Turn out to callouts when you can, and not feel guilty when you cannot
  • Be over 18 and hold a full driving licence
