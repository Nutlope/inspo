Larch House sits at the top of a sloping plot on the edge of Wivenhoe, with the lane to the north and the Colne estuary opening out below the garden to the south. The plan is simple: a twelve-metre timber frame, one room deep at the living end, with every principal room facing the water. The living room is double height under the high end of the mono-pitch roof; a gallery library runs above the kitchen and looks back into it.

The house was built by the current owners over fourteen months and completed in May 2019. It is sold with the full set of Marta Ilves Architects' drawings, the structural calculations, the air-test certificate and the ten-year structural warranty, which has four years to run.

03 · Materials

Six materials, left to weather honestly. Nothing on the house needs painting.

Siberian larch rainscreen

Vertical boards, 145 mm, on a ventilated cavity. Untreated, silvering evenly on the south face after six summers.

Standing-seam zinc roof

Pre-weathered zinc on a warm roof, 430 mm seams, concealed gutters. Rainwater runs to a 3,000 litre tank for the garden.

Lime plaster, unpainted

Three coats of lime on woodwool board throughout. Breathable, soft under raking light, and repaired with a trowel not a tin.

Polished concrete ground floor

Power-floated raft, ground and sealed, with underfloor heating cast in. Same surface continues out onto the south terrace.

Douglas fir joinery

Stair, gallery balustrade rail, kitchen fronts and every internal door, made in Suffolk and oiled, never lacquered.

Triple glazing, aluminium-clad timber

Whole-window value 0.8 W/m²K. The living room screen is one fixed pane and two sliders, 5.9 m high, 2.6 m wide each.

05 · Location

Wivenhoe: a quay, a university, a railway station, and an estuary that empties twice a day.

The house is on Anglesea Lane at the eastern edge of the town, where the last houses give way to the marsh footpath. It is a quiet, unmade lane with six other houses; the estuary path runs along the bottom of the garden to the quay in one direction and to the Alresford creek in the other.

Wivenhoe has two pubs on the water, a bookshop, a good baker, a primary school rated outstanding and the University of Essex campus on foot. Colchester, with its market and the mainline, is fourteen minutes by train.
