The bowl · the only thing we cook

Tantanmen, the way we have made it every day since 2019.

Sesame paste is ground each morning and whisked into a pork and chicken broth until it turns to cream. The chili oil is made on Mondays from three dried peppers and sansho, and rested for a week before it touches a bowl. Noodles are pulled to order, medium thick, with a bite.

What is in it

Six things. Nothing you did not ask for. Pick your spice level at the counter, from 1 (a warm hum) to 4 (we will hand you a towel).

Ticket machine · by the door, coins and notes

Buy your ticket first. It takes about twenty seconds.

The machine is the whole ordering system. There is no waiter, no tablet, no app. One big red button for the bowl, a row of small ones for toppings, and a slot that gives you tickets and your change.

  1. 1

    Put your money in

    Coins and 1,000 yen notes only. The machine does not take cards, transit cards or phones, and it does not break a 10,000 yen note. There is a bank next to the station if you need change.

  2. 2

    Press the big red button

    That is the bowl. Press it once per person. The small buttons underneath are toppings and add-ons; press as many as you like, in any order.

  3. 3

    Take your tickets and your change

    One ticket per item drops into the tray on the right. Keep them together. If you bought a beer, the ticket for it is green.

  4. 4

    Join the line outside, then hand the tickets over

    When you reach the counter, give the tickets to whoever is nearest and say your spice level, 1 to 4. Kaedama and extra chili oil are asked for out loud, not bought at the machine.

    Twelve seats at one counter. Groups are seated as seats free up, so a party of four sometimes eats in two pairs.

Queue times · measured at the door

How long you will stand outside, by the hour.

We count the line every half hour and average the last eight weeks. These are typical waits from joining the line to sitting down. Rain shortens them. Payday and the first warm Saturday of spring do the opposite.

Minutes in line, weekdays, from opening at 11:00 to last ticket at 21:30.

Shortest wait

14:30 to 17:00 on a weekday you will usually walk straight in. That is when the staff eat too.

Longest wait

Saturday at noon, and any weekday at 12:15. Forty minutes is normal. Bring a book or a friend who talks.

Last ticket

The machine locks at 21:30 (19:30 on Sundays). If you are in line before then, you eat. We do not cut the line.

Getting here

Take the Tokyu Den-en-toshi line or the Setagaya line to Sangenjaya. Leave by the south exit, walk along Chazawa-dori for three minutes, turn right after the tea shop with the green awning. We are the second door on the left.

Bicycles: there is a rack by the station. Please do not lean bikes on the neighbours' wall.
