03What a full service includes

People write asking for "a clean, lube and adjust" and mean nine different things by it. Here is exactly what ours is. Every camera that leaves the workshop under a full service has had all nine.

  1. Complete strip of the shutter and body

    Every mechanical assembly comes out. We do not flush a shutter in place and call it serviced.

  2. Ultrasonic clean of all metal parts

    Blades, levers, cams and the escapement go through two baths, then are dried and inspected under a loupe.

  3. Old lubricant removed entirely

    Hardened grease is the reason most slow speeds hang. It is all gone before anything new goes in.

  4. Re-lubricated with modern synthetics

    Four different oils and greases, each chosen for the load and temperature of the part it lands on, applied by needle.

  5. Timed at every speed on the tester

    Curtain travel or blade opening measured on a Kyoritsu tester. We aim for a sixth of a stop and write the results on your ticket.

  6. Rangefinder or mirror alignment

    Focus checked at infinity, three metres and the closest distance, on a collimator, and the screen or rangefinder set to match the film plane.

  7. Light seals and dampers replaced

    New closed-cell foam or wool, cut to the channel. Mirror damper replaced so the foam never reaches the prism.

  8. Cosmetic clean and leatherette re-glued

    Vulcanite lifted edges glued back down. We do not polish brass through the paint unless you ask.

  9. Test roll shot and processed

    A short roll through the camera, developed here, checked for capping, light leaks and focus before it goes back in the box.

05How to send a camera in

Most of our work arrives by post. Four steps, and the only one that takes any time is packing it properly. Nothing is opened, let alone worked on, until you have seen a quote and said yes.

  1. 01

    Write us a ticket

    Email service@ostrandercameraworks.com with the make and model, what it is doing wrong, and your return address. A photograph helps. We reply inside a working day with a job number; put that number in the box.

  2. 02

    Pack it the way it will survive

    Cameras are lost to shipping far more often than to age. Please do all of these:

    • Fire the shutter so it is not stored cocked, and take the film out.
    • Caps on, lens off the body if it comes off, each wrapped separately.
    • A box inside a box, with at least two inches of packing all round the inner one.
    • No batteries, no cases, no accessories we did not ask for.
  3. 03

    Ship with tracking and insurance

    Any carrier is fine. Insure for what it would cost to replace, not what you paid in 1994. We sign for everything and email you the moment it arrives.

  4. 04

    Approve the quote, then wait well

    Within three working days of arrival you get a written quote with the turnaround. Say yes and it joins the queue; say no and we send it back for the cost of postage. You will hear from us when it goes on the bench and again when it comes off.
