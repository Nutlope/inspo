What it measures

Nine readings from one sealed housing.

There is nothing to bolt on and nothing to spin. The wind head listens instead of turning, the rain collector self-drains, and the whole assembly weighs 1.9 kg with cells fitted.

Temperature

Platinum resistance element inside the aspirated shield.

-40 to 65 °C

Humidity

Capacitive polymer sensor, heated to shrug off condensation.

0 to 100 % relative

Pressure

Absolute barometer, corrected to sea level for your elevation.

300 to 1100 hPa

Wind speed

Ultrasonic time of flight across four transducers.

0 to 70 m/s

Wind direction

Derived from the same four transducers, so it never sticks.

0 to 359°, 1° steps

Tipping bucket under a 200 cm² collector, with a debris guard.

0.2 mm per tip

0 to 2000 W/m²

0 to 16

Power and range

Put it where the weather is, not where the socket is.

The S2 needs no mains power and no Wi-Fi at the mast. It talks to a small hub indoors over a sub-gigahertz radio link, and the hub plugs into your router.

24months on a set of cells

Four lithium cells sit in a sealed bay at the base. The solar cell on the collar keeps the pack topped up, so most stations report a battery figure that barely moves in the first year. When it does start to drop, the app gives you six weeks of notice.

Compared with the S1

The S1 shipped in 2022 with cups and a vane. Cups ice up, vanes stick, and both wear out. The S2 removes every moving part except the rain bucket, triples the accuracy of the barometer and reaches five times as far.
