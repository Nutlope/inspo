№ 02 — Inside the studio

A barn, two wheels,
and a long kiln week.

Margit Roth and Owen Kessler met at the Penland School in 2017. Four years later they bought a sagging dairy barn on a north-facing slope in Greenport, NY and turned the milking parlour into a throwing room with three north-light windows and one very old cat.

They throw together, glaze separately, and fire once a month in a soda kiln they built from salvaged firebrick in 2022. Everything you see on this site was made by their four hands.

“We’re not trying to be efficient. We’re trying to make a hundred good plates a month and one or two we’ll remember.”

— Margit, 2026

Margit Roth · Wheel № 02 · May

№ 03 — How it’s made

Five weeks, one cup.

We don’t cast or jigger. Every form starts wet on the wheel and finishes in a kiln we light by hand.

01Clay

Local stoneware

We blend our own body from Hudson Valley fireclay and a small portion of porcelain for tooth. Bagged and aged for three months before it touches the wheel.

02Throwing

On a kick wheel

Forms are pulled in a single sitting — never trimmed back from a thick blank. The mark of the hand stays visible on the inside wall.

03Glaze

Wood-ash & oat slip

Three glazes, all wood-ash based, all mixed in the studio. Applied by hand-dipping, so each piece keeps its own drip line.

04Fire

Five days of soda

Loaded slow, fired slow. We sleep on the studio futon for three nights and ring a bell on the fifth morning when the kiln is finally cool.
