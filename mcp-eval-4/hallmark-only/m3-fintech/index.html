Tax money, moved before you spend it.

When an invoice clears, your tax percentage leaves with it. What stays in the account is genuinely yours to spend.

Start setting aside → How the rate is chosen

Three things happen, and then nothing happens again.

  1. You connect the account you get paid into

    Read-only. Even needs to see money arrive; it never needs to move money out of that account on its own.

  2. You set the percentage you want held back

    One number, yours to pick, changeable whenever your situation changes. A note explaining what usually moves it sits further down this page.

  3. An invoice clears, and the slice leaves with it

    The reserve fills in lumps, exactly the way your income does. When the bill arrives, the money is already sitting in one place with a boring name.

Saving a fixed amount every month assumes a fixed month.

Freelance income does not arrive in equal instalments. Two projects land in March, nothing lands in April, and a standing order for a flat sum bounces at the worst possible moment. A percentage of each payment survives that; a monthly figure does not.

The percentage is a decision, not a default.

Even will not guess your tax rate for you. Where you file, whether you are registered for VAT or its local equivalent, what you can deduct, and how much you have already paid this year all move the number, and none of that is visible from a bank feed.

So the rate is a field you fill in. Set it once, raise it after a good quarter, lower it when the numbers say you can. Every payment that lands afterwards follows the new number.

Rate to confirm with whoever does your return. If you do not have one yet, start high: an over-full reserve is a pleasant problem.

Things Even does not do

It does not file anything
Even holds money back. Submitting a return is still between you, your accountant, and the revenue office.
It does not invest the reserve
Tax money is not risk capital. It sits still until you need it.
It does not chase your clients
Even reacts to payments that arrive. Getting the invoice paid in the first place is a different job, and other tools already do it.

Questions people ask before signing up

Can I get the money back out?

Yes. It is your money in your reserve, and there is no lock on it. It is deliberately one step further away than your spending account, which is the whole point, but a step is not a wall.

What if a client pays me in cash?

Even only sees what reaches the connected account, so cash and anything paid elsewhere has to be added by hand. You can record a payment yourself and the same percentage is applied to it.

Does it work if my income is genuinely tiny some months?

That is the case it is built for. A percentage of a small payment is a small amount, so a thin month sets aside almost nothing rather than overdrawing you to hit a target.

I already have a separate savings account for this.

Then you have the hard part solved and only the discipline left. Even is the transfer you keep meaning to make on the day the payment lands, made without you.

The bill is the same either way. The month it arrives in does not have to be.
