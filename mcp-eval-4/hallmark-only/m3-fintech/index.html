No spreadsheet, no January arithmetic. Built for people whose income arrives in lumps: designers, writers, contractors.

Three things happen, and you do none of them.

Even sits beside the account you already invoice into. It does not replace your bank and it does not file anything for you.

  1. The payment lands

    Even reads the incoming payment on the account you connect. Client name, amount, date. Nothing is moved until the money has actually cleared, so a bounced payment never sets anything aside.

  2. The slice is lifted off the top

    Your rate is applied to the amount that arrived, and that share moves into a separate savings account held in your name. It happens the same day, before the balance has time to look like yours.

  3. The rest is just money

    What is left in the current account is spendable without doing sums first. When the bill comes due, the other account is where you have been paying it from all year.

You set the rate once, and change it when your year changes.

A rate that is roughly right, applied to every invoice, beats a rate that is exactly right applied to none of them. Raise it after a good quarter; lower it when work thins out. The account keeps whatever is already in it.

What Even moves
Your chosen share of each payment that clears, into a savings account in your own name.
What Even leaves alone
Everything else in the current account. Standing orders, card payments and direct debits are none of its business.
What you still do
File your return, and decide the rate. Even is a habit, not an accountant.
Getting the money back out
It is your account. Withdraw any of it, any day, without asking Even first.

A short list of things this will never do.

  • Lock your money away until a date it picked.
  • Guess your tax rate and then present the guess as advice.
  • Move anything on an invoice that has not actually been paid.
  • Send you a notification because you spent money on a Saturday.

Questions people ask before they connect anything.

My income is nothing for two months and then everything at once.
That is the case Even is built for. It works per payment rather than per month, so a quiet March sets aside nothing and a heavy April sets aside plenty. Nothing is ever taken from a balance that no invoice paid for.
What rate should I use?
Even will not tell you, because the honest answer depends on your country, your allowances and your expenses. Your accountant will give you a figure in one line; type it in and forget it.
What if I set aside too much?
Then you have savings. The account is yours, the money is withdrawable, and a year that ends with a surplus in it is not a failure of the tool.
Does it work with the way I already invoice?
Even watches the bank account, not the invoice. Whatever you send the invoice from, if the payment arrives in the connected account, Even sees it.
