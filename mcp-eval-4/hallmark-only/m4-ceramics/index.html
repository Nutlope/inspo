How a piece gets from the wheel to the shelf

Six operations, and most of the time is waiting. The log on the kiln door records every one of them, which is where this page got its ruling.

Elapsed
mostly waiting
Kiln
gas, reduction
Order
never varied

  1. Throw

    Stoneware, wedged and centred, thrown to a wall thickness the piece can carry when it is full.

    wet

  2. Trim and pull

    The foot is cut on the wheel at leather hard, handles pulled and set the same day so they shrink at the same rate.

    leather hard

  3. Dry

    Slowly, under plastic first, then open on the rack. Rushing this is how a handle cracks off in the kiln, so nothing is rushed.

    days, not hours

  4. Bisque fire

    The first firing. Chemically bound water leaves, the clay becomes porous and hard enough to handle wet glaze.

    cone 06, about 1000 °C

  5. Glaze

    Dipped from studio-mixed buckets, wiped back off the foot, stacked on the shelf with room to move.

    by hand

  6. Glaze fire, then cool

    The second firing vitrifies the body and melts the glaze to glass. The kiln then takes as long to cool as it did to climb.

    cone 9, about 1280 °C

Saturdays, a small group, one wheel each

You throw for a morning, we trim and fire what survives, and you come back for it once it has cooled. No experience assumed, and no pretending that everything you make is worth keeping: choose two pieces, we recycle the rest.

The studio is a room with a kiln in it

Not a showroom. There is a wheel, a wall of buckets, shelves of things waiting on the next firing, and a table by the window where the finished work sits until somebody takes it away. If the door is open you are welcome to come in and look, and if a firing is on you will hear it before you see it.
