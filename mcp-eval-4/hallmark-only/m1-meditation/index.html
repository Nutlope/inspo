Sitting still was the wrong ask.

The apps you bounced off were not badly made. They asked for the one thing you were never going to give them: a quiet room, a straight back, and the patience to stay in it while your legs argued. Two weeks in, the streak breaks, and the failure feels like it says something about you.

It does not. Attention has a body attached, and some bodies settle by moving. Tenor takes the same ten minutes and spends them outside, at whatever pace you already walk. The session ends when you get where you were going.

  • No streakNothing is counting the days you missed.
  • No rain soundsThe street is the soundtrack. It is already there.
  • No stillnessEyes open, feet moving, headphones in.

Three beats, and then you are back at your desk.

A voice arrives four or five times in ten minutes. The rest is you and the pavement, which is the part that works.

  1. You leave

    Press start on the pavement, not before. The first minute has no instruction in it at all, which is deliberate: the walk has to become ordinary again before anything can be asked of it.

  2. You walk

    The cues are short and they point outward, at your own steps and at what you can hear. Nothing asks you to close your eyes, empty your mind, or notice your breath, because you are crossing a road.

  3. You arrive

    The session closes itself rather than fading out under a chime, and it hands you one thing to keep. Then the audio stops and the app has no further use for your attention.

Sessions are filed by the walk, not by the mood.

You pick the one that matches the ten minutes you actually have, which is usually the walk you were about to take anyway.

The commute leg
Station to office, or the last stretch home. Written for a route you already know by heart, where the walking takes none of your attention.
Once round the block
For the gap between two calls. Starts and ends at the same door, so nothing has to be planned before you leave.
The long way
A deliberate detour, taken slowly, where the point is the extra streets rather than the arrival.
Corridors and stairwells
Indoors, for the days you cannot get out of the building. Shorter cues, no reference to weather or traffic.

What it does while you walk.

  • Audio only, so the screen stays in your pocket for the whole ten minutes.
  • Sessions download in full, and a session that has downloaded runs without a signal.
  • No notification asks you to come back. You open it when you are already going somewhere.
  • Nothing is scored, ranked, or shown to anyone else.

Ten minutes, from wherever you are standing.

Pick a session, put your headphones in, and walk out. If it does not suit you, you have lost a walk you were going to take anyway.
