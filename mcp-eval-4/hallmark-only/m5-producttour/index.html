Six things you do, from the card reader to the render

One screen and one keystroke each. The frames for this revision are not back from capture yet, so every slot below names the screen that belongs in it rather than showing you something staged.

  1. Point it at the card, walk away

    Drop a folder, a card, or a single file. Reelframe copies, indexes and transcribes while it copies, so the transcript is ready at the same moment the footage is. Nothing is transcoded and nothing is moved out of your folder structure.

    You do: drag the folder onto the window.
    It does: copy, hash, transcribe, list the takes.

  2. Read the transcript, not the waveform

    The transcript is the edit surface. Click a sentence and the clip that holds it is selected on the timeline; scrub the timeline and the transcript follows. You are looking at speech, which is what the cut is actually made of.

    You do: read down the page, click a line.
    It does: select the clip, park the playhead on the word.

  3. Delete a sentence, the cut closes behind it

    Select words, press delete, and the timeline ripples: the gap closes, everything downstream slides, and the audio crossfades by a couple of frames so the join does not click. Undo restores the words and the gap together.

    You do: select the sentence, press delete.
    It does: ripple the timeline, feather the join, keep the original take.

  4. Star the takes you like; the shortlist becomes the sequence

    While you watch, press one key on anything worth keeping. The starred lines collect into a shortlist you can reorder by dragging the lines themselves. When the order reads right, the shortlist is already a sequence: there is no separate assembly step, because the list was the assembly all along.

    You do: press S on a good take, drag lines until the order reads.
    It does: hold the shortlist as a live sequence, in order, with the source ranges attached.

  5. Trim on the timeline when the words are not enough

    Words get you most of the way; frames finish it. J, K and L shuttle, I and O set the in and out, and the trim handles snap to sentence boundaries unless you hold the modifier to ignore them. This is the one place the tour asks you to think in frames.

    You do: park on the join, nudge the handle.
    It does: snap to the sentence, or let go when you tell it to.

  6. Send it out once, with the captions you already wrote

    The transcript you edited is the caption file, so there is nothing to write twice. Export burns them in or writes them beside the video as a sidecar, and the render keeps going while you keep cutting.

    You do: pick burned-in or sidecar, press export.
    It does: render in the background, name the file after the job.

The keys the tour used

J K LShuttle back, hold, shuttle forward.
ISet the in point at the playhead.
OSet the out point at the playhead.
SStar the take under the playhead.
DeleteRipple-delete the selected words.
⌘ ZUndo the words and the gap together.

What this tour did not show you, because Reelframe does not do it

  • No colour grading. Round-trip to whatever you already grade in; Reelframe keeps the source ranges intact so the conform is clean.
  • No motion graphics, no title animator, no plugin market. Titles are text on a track, and that is the whole of it.
  • No cloud requirement. The transcript is produced on your machine and the project is a folder you can zip.
