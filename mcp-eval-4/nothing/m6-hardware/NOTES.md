## What drove the design
The product is ink on paper, so the page is too: one warm paper field, near-black ink, and no third color anywhere, because the restraint itself argues the "nothing to check" promise. Newsreader carries the prose and IBM Plex Mono carries anything machine-shaped (nav, labels, keycaps, the spec sheet), pairing the literary audience with the hardware subject. With remote images banned, the hero product shot is a CSS-drawn landscape writing machine: an e-ink screen holding a draft mid-sentence, a full keycap grid, and "battery: fine" as the entire status bar. The brief gave exactly four facts (screen, keyboard, battery, nothing) and each became a numbered section in that order. Rule 5 shaped section 01 directly: hardware pages want a spec table, and with no given numbers the table became a spec sheet of absences where every value is "none". Both CTAs are drawn as physical keycaps with a pressed active state, tying conversion to the tactile pitch, and hovers snap with no easing to mimic an e-ink refresh. Verification screenshots caused two changes: the section 01 closing line orphaned one word and got a wider measure, and the glare streak washed out its own caption, so pane text moved above the overlay. No aids were used; every decision traces to the brief text, the deliverable rules, or screenshots of my own page.

## Friction
Hardware marketing reaches for numbers (ppi, grams, price) and rule 5 removed them all; making absence itself the spec sheet was the unlock, but it took a while to arrive at.
With no design aids allowed, fold verification fell to headless Chrome screenshots plus sips cropping; it worked, though element box data would have been nicer than eyeballing pixels.
