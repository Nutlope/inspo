If you bounced off the others

Three rules the other apps have. Tenor doesn’t.

NOT THIS

Sit down and don’t move

No cushion, no cross-legged posture, no ten minutes of staring at a patch of wall while your calf goes numb. The session moves because you do. Restlessness stops being the thing you have to defeat first.

NOR THIS

Empty your mind

Attention needs somewhere to go. Tenor puts it on things that are already there: the weight shifting foot to foot, the temperature on your hands, the sound two streets over. The rest is allowed to keep running.

NOR THIS

Find the time first

Ten minutes, doorstep to doorstep. It fits inside a walk you were taking anyway: the coffee run, the station, the loop you do when a meeting ends badly. Nothing new goes in the calendar.

How a session runs

Press play in the doorway. It takes it from there.

01 / STEP OUT

It waits until you’re moving

Nothing is said while you’re still finding your keys. The first cue lands once you have a rhythm going, so the session starts where you actually are rather than where an app assumed you’d be.

02 / FIND THE TEMPO

The voice settles into your stride

Guidance arrives on your beat, not a studio metronome’s. Walk faster and it spaces out. Slow down and it comes closer. When you clearly don’t need anything, it says nothing at all.

03 / COME BACK

The last stretch brings you up

Ending mid-drift on a pavement is no good to anybody. The closing minutes hand you back the traffic, the door, the afternoon, so you arrive as a person who can answer an email.

The idea

It listens for your steps, not your breath.

Breath-led meditation asks you to slow down before you’ve settled, which is precisely the instruction a restless person cannot follow. Walking hands you a rhythm you didn’t have to manufacture. Tenor takes that rhythm as the starting point and builds the session on top of it, so the first minute is already working instead of being spent negotiating with your own body.

Questions

The ones we get from people who quit an app like this before.

I’ve tried Headspace and Calm. Why would this stick?

Because the failure point in those apps is usually the first ninety seconds, when you’re asked to be still and instead you’re cataloguing your inbox. Tenor removes that step entirely. There is no stillness to fail at, and no streak to break: you either went for the walk or you didn’t.

Do I need headphones?

One earbud is plenty, and it’s the arrangement we’d recommend on a street. The audio is mixed to sit under ambient sound rather than replace it, so you keep the traffic, the wind and the person about to pass you on the left.

What if I can only walk indoors, or barely at all?

Corridor is built for a few metres of floor and a lot of turns. Standing Still asks for no distance whatsoever. The point is a rhythm to hang attention on, and shifting weight in one spot counts.

Is it tracking my location?

Sessions run on step rhythm from the device’s motion sensor. Location is off unless you turn it on to map a route, and everything works offline with the sessions downloaded.

Can I try it before paying?

Yes. A set of the sessions above is open with no account and no card, including Out and Back. If ten minutes on your feet turns out not to be your thing, you’ll know by the end of the week.
