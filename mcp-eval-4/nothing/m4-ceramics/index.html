02

from clay to shelf

Roughly three weeks, most of it waiting.

Not a secret process, just a slow one. This is what stands between a bag of clay and the plate on your table.

01

Wedged & thrown

Clay is wedged by hand to knock the air out, then centred and thrown one piece at a time. A plate takes a few minutes; getting the same plate twice takes years.

02

Trimmed & dried

Left overnight to firm up, turned over, and trimmed back to weight. Then a week on the rack, slowly, so nothing warps or splits at the rim.

03

Bisque & glaze

A first firing hardens the piece enough to handle. Glaze is mixed in the studio and dipped, not sprayed, which is why the edges break the way they do.

04

Fired & sorted

Twenty-odd hours to 1260 °C and a full day to cool. Whatever comes out sound goes to the shop; whatever doesn't goes in the shard bucket.

03

A small room, two wheels, one kiln.

Kiln & Co. is a studio, not a brand with a factory behind it. Everything sold here was made in this room, and if a piece has a thumbprint in the foot ring, that is because someone's thumb was there.

We keep the range short and the batches small on purpose. It means we sell out of things, and it means we can answer any question about any piece: which clay, which glaze, which week.

If you only want one bowl, we would rather it were the one you keep.

Repairs are part of it too. Bring a chipped piece of ours back and we will tell you honestly whether it can be saved.
