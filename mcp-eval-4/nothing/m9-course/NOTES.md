## What drove the design
The organizing decision was that a writing course's page should demonstrate the writing it sells, so the hero performs an edit: a red strike through "a soft skill" leaves the front-loaded thesis "Writing is an engineering skill." Two typefaces split the brand's two worlds, Newsreader for prose and JetBrains Mono for labels and structure, set on manuscript paper with one editor's-red accent reserved for marks, numerals and pilcrows. The pain section renders as archetypal reviewer comments ("Could you add a TL;DR?") each with a translation, because senior engineers recognize comment culture faster than any claim about it. With metrics, testimonials and logos off the table, proof became a before-and-after specimen: a flabby draft sentence with its deletions struck, then the rewrite that leads with the ask. The brief's three hard facts (four weeks, two evenings a week, cohorts of twenty) drove the hero fact strip, the eight-evening curriculum grid, and the hand-circled "Twenty" in the apply band. This is the control arm: every decision came from the brief and prior taste, and nothing external was consulted.
