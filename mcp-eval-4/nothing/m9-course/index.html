¶ A writing course for engineers

Writing is a soft skill
an engineering skill.

Your design docs get skimmed. Your proposals get misread. Primer is four weeks of deliberate practice for senior engineers who write constantly and were never taught how.

¶ The symptoms

You've seen the comments.

"Could you add a TL;DR?"

Translation

There is a point, somewhere in section four. Your reader gave up before reaching it.

"LGTM. A few small nits."

Translation

Approved, not read. Every nit sits in the first two paragraphs, because nobody made it further.

"Let's hop on a call to discuss."

Translation

The document was supposed to be the meeting. Instead it scheduled one.

"So we're rewriting everything in Rust?"

Translation

You proposed nothing of the kind. A misread proposal breeds counter-proposals to plans nobody made.

None of this is a reading problem. It's a writing problem.

¶ The curriculum

Four weeks. Eight evenings.
One document at a time.

01

Evenings 1 & 2

The point

Every document exists to get a decision made. Week one is learning to find yours and put it first: the answer-first opening, headings that carry the argument, and a summary you could defend in a hallway.

You leave with

Your current design doc, reopened and front-loaded.

02

Evenings 3 & 4

The design doc

Context without throat-clearing. Alternatives compared honestly instead of dismissed ceremonially. Scope, non-goals, and the failure modes you are choosing to accept, stated in writing.

You leave with

A doc your reviewers can disagree with usefully.

03

Evenings 5 & 6

The proposal

Written for the person who can say yes. The ask in the first sentence, cost next to benefit, objections answered before they're raised, and a summary that survives being forwarded twice.

You leave with

A real proposal, sent to a real decision-maker.

04

Evenings 7 & 8

The edit

Where the writing actually happens. Cut a third without losing meaning. Choose verbs over abstractions. Hunt hedges, passives, and the sentences that make readers feel stupid. Then: editing colleagues without making enemies.

You leave with

A red pen, and the judgment to use it.

¶ The work

Twenty minutes into evening two.

Draft, as submitted

the ask is missing

This document provides something of a high-level overview of some of the potential options that could be considered for the proposed migration of the billing pipeline at some point in the coming quarters.

After the workshop

the ask leads

Move billing to the queue-based pipeline by March. It removes our two worst sources of pager load and costs one quarter of platform time. Decision needed by Friday.

Live edits, out loud, on documents the cohort actually owes someone.

¶ The format

A workshop, not a webinar.

Cohort
Twenty engineers. Few enough that every document gets read aloud and marked up; enough that the arguments are real.
Schedule
Two evenings a week for four weeks. Ninety minutes each: a short seminar, then line edits in groups of four.
Material
Your own documents, start to finish. Bring the design doc you owe your team; redact what you must. Structure is what we're after, and structure survives redaction.
Feedback
Everything comes back marked up twice: once by an instructor, once by peers who, by week three, know how to be useful.

For you if

  • You're a senior engineer, a lead, or staff-adjacent, and the job is mostly persuasion now.
  • Your documents are correct, complete, and unread.
  • Performance feedback keeps spelling the problem "communication".

Not for you if

  • You want grammar drills or a certificate to frame.
  • You haven't written a design doc yet. Come back after your first.
  • You'd rather a tool did the writing. The judgment is the point, and it doesn't delegate.

¶ House rules

Six rules, drilled until they're reflexes.

01

Front-load the decision.

02

Write for the skimmer; reward the reader.

03

One idea per paragraph.

04

Headings should carry the argument alone.

05

Every hedge costs you a reader.

06

If it can be shorter, it isn't finished.

¶ Questions

Asked, answered.

How much time does it take outside the sessions? +

Two to three hours a week, spent rewriting your own documents. The homework is your actual job, done better.

Is it live? Where? +

Live over video, in the evening, so it fits around a working day. Recordings go up the next morning, but the marking-up happens in the room.

My documents are confidential. +

Common, and fine. Redacted versions work well, and so does a parallel document about a stand-in system. Structure transfers; secrets don't have to.

What if I miss an evening? +

One is survivable: watch the recording, then catch up with your edit group. Miss more than that and we'll move you to a later cohort rather than let the course happen to an empty chair.

Does my employer pay for this? +

Usually, through an education budget. We invoice; you forward it. Consider it the cheapest infrastructure your documents will ever run on.
