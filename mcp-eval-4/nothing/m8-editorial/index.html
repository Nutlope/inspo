Footnote No. 87 · Thursday, 6:00 a.m.

Why the bus is late

On bunching, the physics of patience, and the case for promising less.

The bus is not late because the driver is slow, and it is not late because the schedule was drawn by optimists. It is late because lateness compounds. A bus that falls a minute behind meets more waiting passengers at every stop, and every extra boarding makes it a little later still. The bus behind it meets fewer, runs faster, and quietly closes the gap.* Left alone, the two will eventually arrive everywhere together, in a pair, like nervous guests.

The interesting question is what a city does about it. Some agencies pad the schedule until the bus is never late, which is another way of saying it is always slow. Some hold buses at timing points while the passengers stare at an open door.† The clever ones stop promising times altogether and promise a frequency instead: a bus every six minutes, no schedule to miss, nothing to be late against. Which of these your city chose says more about it than the skyline does, and this week we are going to read that choice closely.

*Riders know this as two 14s arriving at once, then nothing for twenty five minutes. It is not bad luck. It is a feedback loop with a schedule.

†If you have ever sat on a stopped bus while nothing visibly happened, you were briefly part of the control system. Thank you for your service.
