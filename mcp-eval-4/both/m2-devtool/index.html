Built for platform teams who own the pipeline and did not write the code that broke it.

  1. A run goes red.

    The pipeline reports a failure, the way it always has. Same runner, same webhook, same red mark in the channel nobody reads on a Tuesday. Sightline starts from that event rather than from a dashboard you have to remember to open.

    Nothing changes in your build. There is no wrapper around your steps and no agent inside the container.

  2. It reads what the run touched.

    A failure is rarely explained by the last hundred lines of output, which is exactly where a human starts because it is cheap. Sightline reads the parts that are expensive to read.

    • Job outputThe whole stream, every step, not the tail that fit on the screen.
    • The diffEvery commit since the last run that finished green, not just the one on top.
    • Resolved depsLockfiles, base images, and the versions your runner actually resolved at start.
    • The runnerMemory ceiling, disk, network egress, and the timeouts the job ran up against.
    • This pipeline’s pastHow this job has failed before, and which of those failures came back.

  3. It writes the probable cause.

    One paragraph, in the order an engineer asks for it: what failed, what changed, and which of those two facts explains the other. Every claim in it links to the exact line it was read from, so the paragraph is a way into the log rather than a substitute for it.

    When the evidence does not separate a real regression from a moving dependency, the finding says so in those words. A confident wrong answer costs more than no answer, and it is the reason engineers stop trusting tools like this one.

  4. You agree, or you correct it.

    Every finding takes a verdict: correct, or wrong and here is what it actually was. Corrections are the only signal Sightline learns your pipeline from, and they stay inside your account.

    A finding you never open is also a signal. If a pipeline’s findings go unread for a fortnight, Sightline stops writing them and tells you it has.

Four things it will not do.

  • It will not replace your logs. It cites them. Every sentence in a finding carries the line it came from, and the line is one click away.
  • It will not run your builds. Read access to output and artifacts is the whole integration. No privileged runner, no step you have to add.
  • It will not page anyone. It writes the paragraph and stops. Your on-call rotation stays where it is and stays yours.
  • It will not score your team. There is no leaderboard, no flakiness ranking by author, and there will not be one.

Attaching it takes three things.

01
A webhook from your CI, pointed at Sightline, firing on run completion.
02
Read access to job output and build artifacts. Nothing that can write.
03
A quiet fortnight while it reads your recent history and learns what green looks like here.

Point it at your noisiest pipeline.

Start with the one that fails for reasons nobody can name. If Sightline cannot explain that pipeline, it has not earned the rest of them.
