For people paid in invoices, not salaries: designers, writers, contractors.

One payment, followed all the way through.

Nothing here happens monthly. Even only ever reacts to money arriving.

  1. The invoice is paid.

    Even watches the account you connect and waits for the credit to clear. No forwarding of PDFs, no marking things off by hand.

  2. Your rate is applied to that payment.

    Not to your month, not to a rolling average of your year. The share you chose, taken from the amount that just arrived.

  3. The set‑aside moves the same day.

    Into a pot held in your name, kept apart from the balance you spend out of. You see the transfer; you do not have to make it.

  4. What is left is genuinely spendable.

    The number in your main account is already after tax. That is the whole product: no mental subtraction, no January discovery.

Some months are five figures. Some are nothing.

Budgeting tools are built for salaries. They ask what you earn a month, then act wounded when the answer is “it depends on whether the studio paid.” Freelance income does not arrive in equal parts, so a rule based on the month is wrong twice: it over‑reserves in a quiet March and under‑reserves in a loud June.

Even never asks the monthly question. A payment either arrives or it does not, and the share only ever comes off what arrived. A month with no work costs you nothing, because there was nothing to take a share of.

Nothing is averaged.
Nothing is projected.
Nothing is taken from a balance that has not moved.

Where the money actually sits.

The pot is boring on purpose. It is a place, not a product.

Held
In your name, not Even’s.
Kept apart
A separate balance from the one your card spends out of.
Reversible
Pull money back whenever you need it. It is yours; a bad quarter is not a lockout.
Your rate
You set the percentage and change it whenever your situation does.
On the record
Every set‑aside is listed against the payment that caused it, and exports as a file.

Then the bill arrives, and it is already paid for.

The point of the pot is the day you have to empty it. When your return is filed, the money has been sitting there since the invoices that generated it were paid, line by line, each one traceable back to the job. You move it out, you pay the bill, and the year starts again at zero rather than at a deficit you have been carrying since spring.

Even does not calculate what you owe and does not file anything. It moves money out of reach and keeps a record of having done it. Your accountant does the rest, with a cleaner statement than usual.

Questions people actually ask.

What if I set aside too much?

Then the pot is bigger than the bill and the difference stays yours. Move it back to your spending balance, or leave it as the start of next year’s reserve.

What if a client pays late, or only half?

Even reacts to money, not to invoices. A part payment gets the same share taken off the part that arrived; the rest is handled when the rest turns up.

Can I change my rate mid‑year?

Yes, and it applies to payments from that point on. Earlier set‑asides stay where they are rather than being recalculated behind your back.

Is this tax advice?

No. Even moves a share of what you are paid into a separate place and keeps a record of it. Deciding the right share, and what you owe, is between you and whoever does your books.

Start with the next invoice you send.

Pick a share, connect the account the money lands in, and then leave it alone. The first payment that clears is the first one you do not have to do arithmetic on.
