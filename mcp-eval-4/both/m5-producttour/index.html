Five steps from rushes to a cut.

No pitch on this page. Just the five things you do inside Reelframe, in the order you do them, with the surface you touch at each one drawn out beside it.

Start the tour Skip to the export

Reading time about 4 minutes. Nothing here needs an account.

An edit is paperwork with pictures in it. These five rows are the paperwork, in order.

  1. Point it at the card.

    Drop a card, a folder, or a drive on the bin and Reelframe reads what is there rather than asking you to describe it. Clips keep the camera’s own names and timecode. Proxies build in the background while you start watching, so the first clip opens before the last one has copied.

    Drag
    a folder onto the bin adds everything inside it, once.
    ⌘ I
    opens the same importer from the keyboard.

  2. Watch once, mark as you go.

    The first pass is the whole job. Play a clip, set an in and an out around the part that works, and it lands in the selects bin under the same name. Nothing is copied and nothing is trimmed: a select is a pair of timecodes pointing at the original, so changing your mind later costs nothing.

    J K L
    shuttle back, hold, and forward, at up to four times speed.
    I / O
    set the in point and the out point where the playhead sits.

  3. Build the cut, then argue with it.

    Selects drop onto the timeline in the order you marked them, which is usually wrong, which is fine. Dragging a clip ripples the ones after it instead of leaving a hole. Dragging the seam between two clips rolls the cut point: one gets longer, the other gets shorter, and the sequence keeps its length. Both are undoable to the frame.

    Drag a clip
    ripple: everything downstream shifts with it.
    Drag a seam
    roll: the cut point moves, the duration does not.

  4. Get the voice above the room.

    Audio sits under the picture on its own lanes with a level line you can pull down by hand. Where music runs under speech, a duck marker pins the music’s level to the dialogue lane, so the music steps back on its own when someone talks and comes back up when they stop. The line is the truth: what you see is what renders.

    Drag the line
    sets the level for that stretch of the clip.
    Alt click
    drops a point so the change stays where you put it.

  5. Send the file, keep working.

    An export is a row in a queue, not a modal that takes the app hostage. Pick a preset, name the file, and keep cutting while it writes. Every row records what it was made from, so re-exporting a sequence after a note is one click rather than a re-guess at the settings.

    ⌘ E
    queues the current sequence with the last preset used.
    Re-run
    repeats a finished row against the sequence as it stands now.

That is the whole application.

There is no sixth step and no advanced mode holding the good parts back. If the five rows above look like the way you already work, the rest of Reelframe is those five rows getting faster as the footage gets bigger.
