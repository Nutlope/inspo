Ten minutes, on foot, eyes open.

Tenor is a meditation app for people who cannot sit still. Every session runs ten minutes and you walk through all of it: to the station, around the block, down to the corner and back.

No cushion. No streak counter. No lying down at the end.

You have already tried sitting still.

Two apps, a cushion bought in January, a fortnight of good intentions, and then a red badge you swipe away every morning. That is not a failure of discipline. Sitting is a posture, and it happens to be the posture you are worst at.

Tenor moves the practice into something you were doing anyway. The attention work is the same work. The sitting is simply gone.

What the ten minutes hold

Five stages, in order, every session. The voice is in your ear for about three of the ten.

  1. before 0:00

    Stand still, briefly

    Twenty seconds on the spot, before a single step. Not to centre yourself: to find the weight sitting in your heels, which is where the rest of the session lives.

  2. 0:00 to 2:00

    Set off at your own pace

    Walk exactly as fast as you were going to walk. Nothing corrects the pace, nothing counts the steps yet, and no one asks you to breathe differently on a public street.

  3. 2:00 to 6:00

    The ground

    Attention goes down to the soles of the feet, one contact at a time. When it wanders off, and it will, the next kerb brings it back for free.

  4. 6:00 to 9:00

    The count

    Steps counted in fours, silently. This is the part built for a restless mind: it gives the busy half of your head a job small enough to hold and dull enough to drop.

  5. 9:00 to 10:00

    Arrive before you stop

    The session ends while you are still moving. The voice goes, you keep walking, and the last minute belongs to the street rather than to the app.

What it needs from you

A phone in a pocket
Screen off, face in, coat or bag. Tenor is audio the whole way through, so there is nothing to look at and no reason to take it out.
One earbud, or none at all
Traffic is part of the session, not an interruption of it. Keep an ear on the road; the audio is mixed to sit under it.
A route you already walk
The station, the school run, the long way round to the kitchen at work. Familiar is better: a route you know needs none of your attention.
Ten minutes you were spending anyway
This is the only claim Tenor makes on the day. Nothing is added to the calendar.

Sessions, by the walk you were taking

Every one of them runs ten minutes.

  • The commute legFrom the front door to the platform. Ends before the train does. 10 min
  • Between two meetingsFor a gap that is not long enough to sit down in, and corridors that are not long enough to think in. 10 min
  • After the call went badlyWalk it down before you answer the next one. Slower count, fewer words. 10 min
  • The block around the buildingOne loop, no destination, nothing to plan on the way. 10 min
  • Terminal to gateRolling luggage, moving walkways, and no stillness available anywhere in the building. 10 min
  • Last thing at nightStreetlights, cold air, and the count taken down to a walking pulse. 10 min

The next ten minutes will happen either way.

Put one earbud in on the way out and let the first session run. If it does not hold you by the fourth minute, walk on without it.
