Everything here is fired twice

The first firing makes the clay permanent. The second one makes the glaze glass. Neither can be hurried, which is most of why a batch is small.

  1. Thrown, then left alone

    A piece comes off the wheel too soft to touch. It dries slowly under plastic, then the foot is turned on the wheel once the clay has gone leather hard.

  2. The first firing

    Dry clay goes into the kiln and comes out porous and hard. From here nothing can be reclaimed, so this is where a piece either continues or goes in the bucket.

  3. Glaze, poured and wiped

    Glaze is mixed in the studio from dry materials and poured over the piece. The foot is wiped clean by hand so it will not weld itself to the kiln shelf.

  4. The second firing

    The kiln climbs overnight and cools for a day. Copper goes turquoise, iron goes rust, and the studio finds out what it made when the door opens.

The wheels are free at weekends

Sessions run in the front room, four wheels and one long table. You leave the piece with us; it is fired twice over the following weeks and you come back for it.

The studio

Kiln & Co is two rooms off a street in Alcântara: the front room with the wheels and the long table, the back room with the kiln, the glaze buckets and the shelves where work waits to be dry enough to fire.

We make in batches because that is what a kiln is. It holds what it holds, it takes a day to climb and a day to cool, and it does not care that a shop would rather have a hundred of the same plate. What comes out is a run of objects that are alike but not identical, and that is the whole argument for buying one of them instead of ten of something else.

The door is open when the light is on. If you want a piece held back, or a set made to a size, write to the studio and we will tell you which firing it can go in.
