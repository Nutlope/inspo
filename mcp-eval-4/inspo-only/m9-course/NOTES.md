## What drove the design
The governing idea, that the page should itself be the document the course promises (a page that survives skimming), came from the brief, but the archive decided its form: formafantasma-com's full record showed a website typeset as an academic document with tracked-caps labels and decimal-numbered sections, and I lifted that anatomy directly for the five numbered sections and the mono section labels. Before the letter/long-document exemplar calls I had no committed macrostructure; gwern and loops.so settled it as a long-document page with serif prose, and loops.so specifically supplied the warm paper, the Newsreader display face, and the roughly 700px document measure I used for the prose column. The recommend call's evidence packet measured education at 79 percent grotesk-sans, which I treated as gravity to defy on purpose: serif for prose because this is a course about writing, IBM Plex Mono for labels and specimen edits because the audience is engineers, a pairing no single exemplar showed. The editor's-red accent was picked from the evidence packet's warm anchors (Stanford's #b4040c, #b85546) and given a job the genre suggested: strikethrough-and-insertion editing marks, so the hero visual is a design-doc paragraph being edited rather than a product mock, which also kept me clear of the no-invented-metrics rule. The bold-skim spine (read only the bold and each section still argues) restates the course's own lesson and doubled as the margin-note device, which is formafantasma's annotation register repurposed. The recommend call's actual pick, bento-grid, I rejected; its own shortlist and rationale showed it was leaning on off-genre hits, so for the top-level pick the archive mostly confirmed what the exemplar calls had already decided.
