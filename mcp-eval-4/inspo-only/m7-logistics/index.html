Why it exists

A held container does not cost you once. It costs you every day it sits.

  • Storage and demurrage accrue daily, whoever made the mistake.
  • An exam can add days or weeks, and your delivery window does not move with it.
  • A broker on retainer solves this for companies with a broker budget. Yours has payroll.

Most holds start as paperwork: a code on a watch list, a vague description, a value that does not reconcile. Software can read for those before customs does.

The flag library

The codes and mistakes that get shipments held.

Manifest keeps a library of the patterns customs actually acts on, and reads every entry against it before filing.

Section 301 exposure

Duty risk

Codes on active Section 301 lists change the duty math by origin. Manifest names the list and the current treatment before you file.

AD/CVD scope match

Hold risk

Antidumping and countervailing orders carry deposits and scrutiny. Lines that look in scope are flagged with the order to check.

Valuation out of line

Exam trigger

Declared values that do not reconcile with the invoice or the terms of sale invite an exam. Manifest does the arithmetic first.

Vague descriptions

Exam trigger

“Parts”, “gifts”, and “samples” get containers opened. Descriptions are rewritten to say what the goods actually are.

Origin gaps

Hold risk

Every line needs a country of origin that matches its marking. Gaps and mismatches surface before the entry goes out.

Agency triggers

Hold risk

Some codes bring another agency to the table: FDA, USDA, FCC. Manifest flags which apply, so the extra filing is not a surprise.

FAQ

Fair questions.

Do I still need a customs broker?

For routine entries, Manifest prepares and files the paperwork and you remain the importer of record. Some situations genuinely call for a licensed broker or a trade attorney, and when one does, Manifest says so plainly rather than guessing on your behalf.

What do I need before my first entry?

Your importer of record number, a customs bond, and the shipment’s documents. If any of those words are new, setup walks you through getting each one; none of them requires a broker to obtain.

Which shipments can it handle?

Routine ocean and air entries for commercial goods: the kind a small importer files month after month. Unusual entry types and disputes are exactly the cases it will tell you to take to a professional.

What happens if a shipment is held anyway?

The entry, its documents, and every notice about it live in one record, so you respond with an organized file instead of a scramble through inboxes. Holds are resolved by fast, complete answers; Manifest’s job is having them ready.
