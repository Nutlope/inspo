Everything to write. Nothing to check.

Vellum is a pocket e-ink tablet for first drafts: a screen like good paper, a keyboard with real keys, and a battery that lasts a week. No feed, no pings, no inbox. Your words, and the quiet to finish them.

00 · the_argument

Every notes app is a door to every other app. Vellum is a room with a desk, a chair, and your manuscript. The door locks from the inside.

01 · the_screen

A screen that behaves like paper.

Electronic ink reflects the room instead of shining at you. Your page has no glow, no glare, no flicker: the draft sits on the surface like print and stays readable at full noon, on a park bench, through sunglasses.

Hours in, your eyes feel like they have been reading a book. Because, optically speaking, they have.

matte finish readable in direct sun zero glow

02 · the_keys

Keys that push back.

Glass takes dictation grudgingly. Vellum’s keyboard is the real thing: sculpted caps, honest travel, and a quiet thock that tells your fingers the letter landed. Type by feel. Watch the sentence, not your thumbs.

One red key starts a new paragraph. You will wear it out.

full-travel switches sculpted caps quiet enough for a library

03 · the_battery

Charge it on Sunday. Forget it exists.

E-ink spends power only when the page changes, and a page of prose changes slowly. One charge covers a week of drafting: seven mornings, one cable, no battery anxiety.

The charger stays home. That was the idea.

a week per charge charges over dinner

04 · the_quiet

Vellum has no browser, no apps, no accounts, no little red numbers. Nothing arrives unless you type it. There is nothing to check. We checked.
