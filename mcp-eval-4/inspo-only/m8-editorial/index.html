Why the bus arrives in threes

On bunching, headways, and the difference between a punctual bus and a reliable one.

A bus route is a queue pretending to be a timetable. When one bus falls slightly behind, more passengers accumulate at every stop ahead of it, so boarding takes longer and it falls further behind.3 The bus following it meets thinner crowds, runs faster, and closes the distance. Nothing here is broken. Each driver is doing the job; the route is obeying its own arithmetic. This is why the schedule taped to the pole at your stop is closer to fiction than to promise.

The remedy is known, unglamorous and mostly political. Stop chasing the timetable and start managing the gap: hold buses to even headways,4 open every door for boarding, and give the traffic signal to the bus that is late rather than the cars that are not. Agencies that make this turn quietly stop publishing arrival times and start publishing frequencies. The rider stops checking the schedule at all, which is the point. In a city that works, the bus is not an event. It is a condition.

Notes

  1. 3Transit planners call it bunching. It is a feedback loop, and it lives in the arithmetic of any route with stops; no villain required.
  2. 4The headway is the gap between one bus and the next. Riders experience even headways, not punctual buses, as reliability.

§ 03

The format

The format is the promise

Most newsletters grow by adding. Footnote holds its shape, because the shape is what you are paying for.

01

One subject

No digest, no link dump, no "ten things worth your time". A single question about how a city works, opened carefully and actually closed.

02

Fifteen minutes

Long enough for the mechanism, short enough for a commute. When a subject needs forty minutes, it becomes two letters, and says so.

03

Documents first

Budgets, ordinances, transcripts, tariff schedules, procurement filings. Then the people who wrote them. Then a walk to look at the street the paperwork describes.

04

Notes that earn their keep

Jargon gets a footnote, not a free pass. Every claim that sounds convenient gets its source pinned underneath, where you can check it.
