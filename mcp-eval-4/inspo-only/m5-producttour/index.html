Step 01 · Import

Drop the card on the window. Nothing else.

Drag a card, a folder, or a single file onto the bin. Reelframe reads it where it sits, so there is no copy step and no waiting screen before you can start scrubbing. Proxies build behind you while you work.

Clips arrive grouped by the day and camera they came from, with their original names intact, because that is what your notes refer to.

Step 02 · Cut

Mark in, mark out, drop it on the line.

The timeline is a ripple timeline by default: pull a clip out and the ones after it close the gap, so a rough assembly stays an assembly rather than a field of holes.

Trims happen under the playhead with the keyboard, and every edit point shows both sides of the join while you drag it. Undo goes all the way back, per sequence.

Step 03 · Sound

Dialogue up, everything else out of its way.

Select the dialogue track and the music ducks to it automatically, on a curve you can grab and reshape rather than a fixed preset. Room tone fills the gaps you cut.

The meters are on the same screen as the picture, so you are never mixing in a window you had to go looking for.

Step 04 · Titles

Type on the frame you are looking at.

Titles are edited on the picture, not in a dialog box. Drag the block where you want it, and the safe areas show themselves only while you are moving something.

Save a title once and it becomes a style: change the style later and every title in the sequence follows, including the ones you already rendered a preview of.

Step 05 · Export

Queue it, keep cutting.

Exports run in the background against the same project you are still editing, so a render never takes the app away from you. Queue several versions of the same sequence at once and they inherit whichever cut was current when you added them.

Every export writes a plain sidecar next to the file listing the sources it used, which is the thing you will want in six months.

Where the tour ends

What this page showed

One sequence, start to finish: ingest, assembly, mix, titles, render. That is the shape of most days in the app, and it is the part we think you should judge it on.

What it did not

Colour grading, multicam, captions and the collaboration model all have their own tours. They are linked from the footer rather than crammed in here.
