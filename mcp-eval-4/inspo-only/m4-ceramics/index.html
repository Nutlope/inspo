How it is made

Clay to cupboard, without leaving the room.

The whole sequence happens on one floor: two wheels, a wedging bench, a glaze corner, and a kiln we load ourselves. Nothing is outsourced, which is why a form takes a fortnight and not an afternoon.

01

Wedge

The clay is cut, slammed and spiralled by hand until there is no air left in it. Air is what cracks a pot in the fire.

02

Throw

Centred, opened, pulled up in three passes. The wall thickness is judged by fingertip, not by gauge.

03

Trim

Leather-hard, back on the wheel upside down. The foot ring is cut here, and the maker's mark goes in.

04

Bisque

A slow first firing turns clay into ceramic and makes it thirsty enough to take glaze evenly.

05

Glaze & fire

Dipped, wiped clean at the foot, then fired hot. What comes out is the piece you get, flaws included.

Weekends

Two hours at the wheel, and something to take home.

Small groups, one wheel each, aprons provided. You throw, we trim and fire, and the piece is ready to collect a few weeks later. No experience assumed and none pretended.

The studio

A garage door, a kettle, and a lot of dust.

We are on a side street in Marvila, east of the centre. If the door is up, the studio is open: come in, look at the shelves, ask what is in the kiln. If it is down, we are probably glazing.
