The chair was the problem, not your attention span.

Divided attention is the method here, not a compromise. The feet keep the count, so there is always something to come back to.

Every app you have tried opens by asking you to stop moving. That is the hardest part of the practice and it arrives first, before you have any reason to trust the thing asking. Ten minutes later you have negotiated with your own legs and lost, and the app has quietly filed the loss under a streak.

Tenor starts from the other end. You are already walking to the station, the meeting, the school gate, the shop that closes at six. The session rides along with the walk you were taking anyway. Nothing about your morning has to move to make room for it.

Five sessions. All of them ten minutes.

Pick by where you are, not by how you feel. Feelings are the thing you are going out to look at; they make a poor filter for choosing.

Every session runs to the same length, so the choice is only ever about where your feet are.

How the ten minutes runs.

  1. Leaving 0:00 to 2:00

    One instruction, then quiet. Match your breath to the steps you are already taking; nobody asks you to slow down, and the first two minutes are allowed to be as distracted as they are.

  2. Counting 2:00 to 7:00

    Five minutes of counting footfall in fours. The count goes missing, and it will go missing, and the next left foot starts it again. That returning is the whole exercise; the counting is only the excuse for it.

  3. Arriving 7:00 to 10:00

    The count drops away and you get three minutes to look at the street you have been walking down. The voice does not come back. The session ends where you happen to be.

Things Tenor will not do.

No streak.
Missing Tuesday is not a debt, and Wednesday does not begin with an apology. There is no counter in here for you to protect.
No chime at the end.
The voice stops. You keep walking. A bell in a crowded street is a way of telling everyone around you that you were meditating.
No rainfall, no forest, no whale.
Ambient sound competes with a bus and loses. What you hear underneath the voice is the street you are actually in.
No reminder at eight in the evening.
You already know when you walk. An app guessing at it is a notification, not a practice.
No circle to breathe along with.
Screen off, phone in a pocket, eyes on the crossing. Anything you have to watch is a session you are doing sitting down.

Before you go out.

  • Headphones are optional. The voice is sparse enough to follow through one ear, and quiet enough to leave both out on a slow street.
  • It runs with the screen off and the phone in a pocket. Nothing on the page needs looking at once the session starts.
  • Indoors counts. Stairwell exists for the days the weather wins and for offices with a long enough corridor.

Ten minutes is the length because it is the length of a walk you were already taking. Longer sessions exist in other apps, and that is where they should stay.

Put your shoes on.

Read the five sessions again

Tenor is a walking meditation app: five sessions, ten minutes each, written for pavement, stairwells and platforms rather than for a cushion. It was made by people who kept failing at sitting still and decided to stop treating that as the failure.
