Edit the words. The picture follows.

Reelframe is a video editor you drive from the transcript. What follows is the whole tour: the five things you actually do in it, in the order you do them.

Start the tour or skip to sending it out

  1. Land the footage

    Drop the card, the second camera and the recorder in together. Reelframe reads the sound on each one and lines them up against each other, so the first thing you see is a synced stack rather than four loose files waiting to be matched by hand.

    Sources stay untouched on disk

  2. Cut on the transcript

    The interview arrives as text beside the picture. Delete a sentence and the clip goes with it. Move a paragraph and the cut moves. This is where a long interview becomes a short one, and it happens at reading speed, because reading is the part you were doing anyway.

    Every edit is reversible in the text

  3. Shape the sound

    Voice first, everything else under it. Reelframe holds the dialogue at a level you set once and ducks the music beneath it wherever the two overlap, so a change in the cut does not send you back to redraw the volume by hand.

  4. Grade, title, caption

    The finishing passes stack on the shot rather than replacing it: a grade, a lower third, captions burned from the transcript you already edited. Turn any pass off and the shot underneath is exactly what the camera handed you.

    Captions come from stage 2, not a second pass

  5. Send it out

    One cut, several shapes. Reelframe reframes the same edit for wherever it is going and writes the caption file beside it, so the vertical version is not a second project you have to keep in step with the first.

    The tour ends here, and so does the edit

Things the tour did not show you

  • There is no effects browser. If a look is worth having, it belongs in the grade.
  • There is no render queue to babysit. The timeline plays at full quality while you work.
  • There is nothing to configure before stage 1. The five steps above are the product.
