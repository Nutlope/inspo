From the bench

One command, one merge, one row in the ledger.

The pipeline lives in your repo as a single shipline.toml. Plan it locally, watch the canary in your terminal, and reach for rollback the same way you reach for undo. No web console required.

When the deploy lands, it carries its author, its blast radius, its risk class, and the diff that produced it. The on-call who gets paged at 03:17 can read the row, not guess.

From repo to row, in three

Adopt it in an afternoon.

  1. Step 01

    Connect

    Install the GitHub or GitLab app, point at one repo. Shipline reads your existing CI config, discovers stages, and proposes a shipline.toml you can edit in a PR. Nothing breaks; nothing is replaced.

  2. Step 02

    Codify

    Decide what counts as risk. Schema migrations, payment paths, jobs that hold a lock. Shipline turns those into policy and asks for the right reviewers, the right canary, the right gate — automatically, every time.

  3. Step 03

    Ship

    Open a PR. The reviewer pings the author with diffs, not drivebys. Merge it. Watch the canary, read the ledger, and when something rolls back the next morning you'll know which line of which commit owns it.

What we shipped this year

Three things we’re opinionated about.

  • Section A

    Reviews that come from the diff, not the team chat.

    Shipline reads the PR. It flags the missing null check, the new SQL that scans, the migration that's missing a rollback, and the public API that just changed in a non-backward way. Your reviewers see one fewer trivial comment and one more second-order question.

    A
  • B

    Section B

    Canaries you trust because they tell on themselves.

    Promote at 5%. Shipline holds the line and tracks the signals you decided matter — p99, error rate, queue depth, the budget your platform team set last quarter. If anything regresses past the floor, the rollback is the default. Promotion is the opt-in.

  • Section C

    A ledger that survives the original committer.

    Every deploy is a row. The row carries author, reviewer, commit, blast radius, gate decisions, and a 60-second timeline of the canary. Six months later, when the on-call opens the ledger at 03:17, the answer is in the row — not in the chat thread that's archived.

    C

Manifesto Nº 02 · May 2026

Deploys aren’t events. They’re prose.

Most deploy tools optimise for the wrong moment — the green check. A deploy is everything around it: who wrote it, who waved it through, what the canary said, and the version that quietly replaced it three hours later. Shipline writes that down so it can be read.

— Eng, Shipline
